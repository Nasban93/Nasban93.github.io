Chapter 3
Dirty Data + MDM
10x Rule is guaranteed
🧠
Memory Trick: "IOCD" → "I Often Create Disasters" Incomplete · Outdated · Correct? No → Incorrect · Duplicated. Dirty data = bad decisions.
10x
THE RULE
The 10x Rule — Always mention this first! It costs 10 times more to fix dirty data AFTER it enters the system than to prevent it from entering in the first place. Prevention is always cheaper than cleanup.
💡 A hospital enters a patient's wrong birthdate. Fixing it after records are linked costs 10x the original entry effort.

🦠 Characteristics of Dirty Data

❌ Incomplete
Missing fields — customer record with no email or phone.
⏰ Outdated
Old addresses, discontinued products, former employees still listed.
❌ Incorrect
Typos, wrong values, mislabeled data — "Mhmd" instead of "Mohammed."
👥 Duplicated
Same customer entered twice with slightly different names.

✅ Solution: MDM

🏛️ Master Data Management (MDM)
Creates ONE single, trusted, authoritative version of core data — customers, products, suppliers. Breaks down data silos so every department sees the same truth.
💡 Walmart's MDM means every store, website, and warehouse share one product database. No duplicates, no conflicts.
Impact of Dirty Data: Flawed analytics → poor decisions → regulatory fines → lost revenue. Dirty data is expensive!

Chapter 8
Resolving Channel Conflict in E-Commerce
BOPIS is the headline answer
🧠
Memory Trick: "BUL" → Buy Online, Unify Pricing, Link Logistics BOPIS · Unified Pricing · Logistical Alignment. Define conflict first, then list the 3 solutions.
⚠️ What is Channel Conflict?
When a company's online store competes with its own physical stores — causing internal tension over pricing, inventory, and customer ownership. Stores lose sales to the company's own website.
💡 Nike's own website undercuts Nike store prices → store managers are furious. That's channel conflict.
🏪 BOPIS — The #1 Answer!
Buy Online, Pick Up In Store. Drives online shoppers back into physical locations where they often buy more on impulse. Removes pure price competition by adding immediacy.
💡 Target: order online at night, pick up in 2 hours. 40% of BOPIS customers buy something extra in-store.
Most Powerful Solution
💲 Unified Pricing Strategy
Same price online and in-store eliminates "showrooming" — where customers browse in-store but buy online for a lower price.
💡 Apple charges $999 for iPhone 16 everywhere — Apple Store, apple.com, all the same price.
Stops Showrooming
🔗 Logistical & Inventory Alignment
Integrate digital and physical channels so both share inventory, fulfillment, and customer data — they cooperate instead of compete.
💡 Zara's system: if online inventory runs low, it pulls from nearby store stock — one unified pool.
Shared Infrastructure

Chapter 10
Reasons for Replacing Legacy Systems
Lead with maintenance cost
🧠
Memory Trick: "MILCM" → "Money Issues Leave Companies Modern" Maintenance Costs · Integration Obstacles · Lack of Staff · Cloud Alternatives · Modern Business Needs
💸 High Maintenance Costs
Older systems need rare specialized skills and expensive hardware. Keeping them alive costs more than replacing them.
💡 The US government still ran COBOL systems in 2020 — paying $400/hr for COBOL programmers while younger devs make $80/hr.
Lead With This
🔌 Integration Obstacles
Legacy systems can't connect with modern cloud services, mobile apps, or APIs. They are hardwired islands.
💡 A bank's 1980s mainframe can't connect to a modern mobile banking app without expensive custom "bridges."
Can't Connect
👴 Lack of Qualified Staff
IT professionals trained in COBOL, FORTRAN are retiring. The talent pool is shrinking, creating critical knowledge gaps.
💡 When a COBOL expert retires, nobody left can fix the system — it becomes a ticking time bomb.
Dying Expertise
☁️ Cloud Alternatives (SaaS)
Modern SaaS systems offer lower costs, better scalability, automatic updates, and no hardware overhead.
💡 Replace on-premise SAP with Salesforce cloud → 60% lower IT costs, updates happen automatically.
Modern Solution
📱 Modern Business Needs
Legacy systems can't support real-time analytics, big data, mobile access, or the high transaction volumes required today.
💡 An airline's 1970s booking system crashes on Black Friday because it was never designed for 10M simultaneous users.
Can't Keep Up

Chapter 13
Project Manager Characteristics
End with the Project Triangle!
🧠
Memory Trick: "PC CRM" → "PMs Create Clear Risk Management" Planning · Coordinating · Communication · Risk Management · Monitoring & Controlling
📋 Planning
Defines scope, identifies milestones, allocates budget, creates the formal Project Plan that governs everything.
💡 Building a new e-commerce website: PM creates a 6-month roadmap with sprints, deliverables, and budget checkpoints.
🔧 Coordinating
Assigns tasks to team members and manages human, financial, and technical resources to ensure timely delivery.
💡 PM assigns designers the wireframes, developers the backend, and QA the testing schedule — keeping all 3 in sync.
📢 Communication
Reports status, milestones, risks, and issues to all stakeholders consistently. Poor communication = lost stakeholder trust.
💡 Weekly Slack updates + monthly board reports. If the PM goes silent, executives panic and micromanage.
⚠️ Risk Management
Continuously identifies threats (scope creep, staff shortages, tech failures) and implements mitigation plans before they escalate.
💡 PM sees developer turnover risk → cross-trains 2 backup devs before the key dev quits. Crisis avoided.
📊 Monitoring & Controlling
Tracks actual progress against the plan continuously and takes corrective action whenever the project drifts off schedule, over budget, or out of scope.
💡 Dashboard shows project is 2 weeks behind → PM adds resources and cuts non-essential features immediately.

⭐ The Project Triangle — Always Conclude With This!

Scope · Time · Cost — Always Linked

  • Expand Scope → must extend Time OR increase Cost (or both)
  • Cut Cost → must reduce Scope OR extend Time
  • Shrink Time → must cut Scope OR increase Cost
  • Every PM decision is a trade-off within this triangle
💡 Client says "add 10 new features" (scope up) → PM says "that will cost $200K more OR take 3 more months" — the triangle in action.
Chapter 14
Discrimination in Social Media Recruiting
AI Bias → Privacy → Governance
🧠
Memory Trick: "APG" → "AI Picks, Governance Guards" AI Bias · Privacy Violations · Governance & Code of Ethics
🤖 AI Bias & Discriminatory Algorithms
ML models inherit historical biases. If past hiring skewed toward certain groups, the algorithm learns to replicate those patterns — systematically disadvantaging protected groups.
💡 Amazon's AI recruitment tool was scrapped in 2018 — it downgraded resumes mentioning "women's" (e.g., women's chess club) because it trained on a decade of male-dominated hires.
Technical Root Cause
🔒 Privacy Violations & Ethical Concerns
Harvesting social media profiles without consent violates digital ethics and civil rights. Accessing religion, family status, or political views visible online introduces illegal bias into hiring.
💡 A recruiter sees a candidate is pregnant from Instagram photos and quietly rejects her — illegal discrimination that would never happen in an interview room.
Legal + Ethical Violation
🏛️ Governance & Code of Ethics
Organizations must implement a Code of Ethics covering AI in recruiting: regular algorithmic audits for bias, clear accountability, and transparent communication to candidates about data use.
💡 IBM's HR team runs quarterly AI audits — if their screening model rejects a protected group 20% more than average, they halt and retrain it before continuing.
